As late November brings crisp seaside breezes, Pohang becomes one of Korea's most enticing coastal getaways. Just two hours and twenty minutes from Seoul via the KTX bullet train, it is easily manageable as a quick weekend trip. Late autumn marks the beginning of the seasonal Guryongpo gwamegi harvest, alongside clear skies ideal for exploring the sensational Space Walk above Yeongil Bay. 🐾 Hermi's route

📍 Space Walk at Hwanho Park

Perched inside Hwanho Park, Space Walk is a walkable art sculpture resembling a massive outdoor rollercoaster track. As you ascend its 333-meter steel pathway, panoramic vistas of Yeongildae Beach and the East Sea open up directly beneath your feet. The thrilling height might test anyone wary of edges, but the breathtaking horizon rewards every step. Please note that access is strictly closed during heavy rain or strong coastal gusts, so verifying current weather conditions beforehand is essential.

📍 Homigot Sunrise Square

Located at the geographic tiger-tail tip of the Korean peninsula, Homigot is an iconic landmark revered for its sunrises. The giant bronze 'Hand of Harmony' rising from the ocean pairs with a reciprocal hand statue on the plaza, crafting a deeply evocative view. Photographers gather at dawn to capture the golden sun fitting neatly between the sculpture's outstretched fingers. Since coastal breezes turn quite frigid from late autumn into winter, bundling up in a windbreaker and thermal layers is a must.

📍 Guryongpo Modern Culture and History Street

Tucked behind Guryongpo Port lies an atmospheric historic street lined with preserved Japanese-style wooden houses from the early 20th century. Propelled to fame as the central filming location for the hit K-drama 'When the Camellia Blooms', it has become a cherished photo stop for travelers. Don't miss climbing the prominent stone stairs at the entrance for the famous signature portrait looking down over the port harbor. The neighborhood is dotted with retro teahouses and nostalgic confectioneries ideal for a relaxed stroll.

📍 Guryongpo Gwamegi & Pohang Mulhoe

Beginning in mid-November, gwamegi—herring or Pacific saury naturally frozen and thawed repeatedly in East Sea winter breezes—takes center stage. Wrap glossy slices of gwamegi in crisp winter cabbage, kelp, and dried gim with garlic, chives, and vinegared red pepper sauce for an astonishingly savory, chewy delicacy. In addition, Pohang-style mulhoe, featuring freshly caught fish tossed with spicy red pepper broth and crushed ice slush, offers an invigorating contrast you cannot skip.

📍 Pohang Jukdo Traditional Market

As the largest traditional market along Korea's eastern coast, Jukdo Market buzzes with contagious maritime energy. Its expansive seafood zone overflows with live snow crabs, giant octopuses, and fresh seasonal sashimi served at very reasonable rates. Deeper inside the alleys, budget-friendly food rows serve steaming bowls of hand-pulled sujebi dough flakes and barley rice platters beloved by locals. It is also the premier shopping destination to pick up vacuum-sealed gift boxes of dried seafood and gwamegi before heading home.
