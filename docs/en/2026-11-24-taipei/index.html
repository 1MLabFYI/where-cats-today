[Where to, Meow?] Taipei Winter Travel Guide: Taipei 101, Jiufen, New Taipei Christmasland & Local Bites

If you are looking for an easy winter getaway, Taipei is a top pick. From November to February, temperatures hover around 18–22°C, making outdoor sightseeing comfortable. During the winter season, grand light illuminations like New Taipei City Christmasland bring festive magic to the city nights. Here is the curated Taipei itinerary recommended by Hermi and Nyami.

🐾 Hermi's route

📍 Taipei 101 Observatory

Taipei 101, the city's iconic skyscraper, offers the ultimate vantage point over Taipei. From the indoor observatory on the 89th floor, visitors enjoy panoramic 360-degree night views of the cityscape. Be sure to check out the massive tuned mass damper located inside the upper floors. Arriving right before sunset lets you experience both daylight vistas and evening lights. Booking tickets online in advance is highly recommended to skip long lines.

📍 Jiufen Old Street

Jiufen Old Street is famous for its atmospheric narrow lanes adorned with red lanterns. Walking up Shuqi Road reveals cozy teahouses and traditional dessert shops on either side. As evening falls and the red lanterns light up, Jiufen transforms into a magical fairytale setting. Wear comfortable walking shoes as the village features steep stairs and stone steps. Direct buses or day tours from central Taipei reach Jiufen in about an hour.

📍 New Taipei City Christmasland

Held annually from mid-November to early January around Banqiao Station, this is Taiwan's premier winter illumination festival. Highlighting the event is a massive 3D light projection show on the New Taipei City Hall building alongside themed light tunnels. Located directly above Banqiao MRT Station, it offers effortless access for travelers. Illumination begins daily at 5:30 PM, accompanied by pop-up booths and food stalls. Visiting on a weekday evening provides a more relaxed photo experience compared to busy weekends.

📍 Must-Eat Taipei Treats (Beef Noodles & Boba)

No visit to Taipei is complete without savoring a bowl of Taiwanese beef noodle soup. You can choose between a clear, delicate broth (Qingdun) or a rich, spicy braised broth (Hongshao). Follow up your meal with a cup of fresh bubble milk tea packed with chewy tapioca pearls. Different eateries offer unique flavor balances, allowing you to customize sweetness and ice levels. Popular restaurants often see queues during peak hours, so arriving slightly early is advised.

📍 Raohe Street Night Market

Alongside Shilin, Raohe Street Night Market is one of Taipei's most beloved evening destinations. Right at the entrance, vendor stalls serve crisp black pepper pork buns (Hujiaobing) fresh out of clay ovens. The straight-line layout makes strolling past vibrant food stalls straightforward and fun. Operating from around 5:00 PM until late night, it is an ideal spot for night-owl foodies. Most vendors accept cash only, so carrying small bills is highly recommended.
